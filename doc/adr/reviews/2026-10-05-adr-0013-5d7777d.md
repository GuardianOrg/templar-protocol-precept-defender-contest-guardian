## Tradeoff assessment

The strongest counterargument is manual-only refresh: it avoids a persistent authorized writer, reduces collisions with stale-price experiments, and requires less host-specific configuration. It is especially persuasive while deployment provenance, ownership, or operation recovery remains unresolved. The ADR fairly retains it as the fallback rather than portraying scheduling as mandatory.

For repeated frontend development, the selected dedicated two-method path provides working-hours convenience without adopting generic unattended trading. The real tension is convenience versus operational coordination and custody: a schedule reduces repetitive operator work but adds a standing signer-bearing process, partial-cycle effects, and manual recovery obligations. Another deliberate tension is narrower outcome custody versus transaction-history completeness: the marker conservatively stops uncertainty, but does not provide a full durable transaction ledger. Both limits are disclosed rather than hidden behind an automatic recovery mechanism.

The production feed/keeper alternative could address real observations, redundancy, funding, and TTL management, but cannot justify treating renewed mock prices as economic data. Keeping those obligations under ADR 0008 avoids conflating disposable development freshness with production oracle liveness.

## Security and maintenance assessment

The architectural boundary is supported by the source: manifest and network/identity pins, exact registered assets, unchanged source prices, RPC-derived timestamps, and only `set_data`/`refresh` sends. Marker creation and fsync precede each send; exact readback precedes marker removal; an existing marker blocks manifest/network work. Argument parsing precedes the cycle, so informational help and unsupported flags do not reach operational work.

The authority boundary is appropriately qualified. `_driver_lock` serializes cooperating local instances; neither source checks nor separate adapter/runtime reads provide atomic chain-state protection. Another authorized writer or malicious same-owner process remains outside that protection. The ADR therefore requires exclusive coordination, trusted tooling and ownership, and separately verified deployment provenance. It does not promise safe unattended mainnet operation.

Recovery deliberately favors refusal over availability. A CLI failure can follow submission; unresolved custody stops subsequent sends until an operator records disposition. A twelve-publication/four-refresh cycle is not atomic, and completed earlier sends survive later refusal. Non-restarting, non-persistent working-hours scheduling does not imply continuous freshness or catch-up. The host files match these configuration claims; loaded-unit activity and lingering are reported prior observations, not independently re-measured in this review.

There is no interface or contract migration proposed. ADR 0007 remains Proposed; ADR 0008 remains Accepted and its production keeper/TTL obligations are neither superseded nor satisfied here. The frozen ADR remains Proposed. No evidence correction is required.

## Consequence simulations

1. **A fresh source timestamp and an unrelated timestamp occupy the same adapter bucket.** For publications at `1800000010/20/30`, the accepted raw timestamp `1800000020` may legitimately project to `1800000000` at resolution 300. An unrelated `1800000015` must fail despite projecting to the same bucket. `_verify_adapter` checks raw membership, age/drift, expected price, and exact bucket projection (`refresh_spoon_prices.py:523–541`); the offline assertions exercise both outcomes. The pinned MedianLow path preserves the selected source timestamp. This closes the rounded-timestamp ambiguity without inventing a second price-conversion implementation.

2. **An authorized writer changes a source during the cycle.** A changed `(price, timestamp)` pair before publication aborts before that send. A later change before runtime refresh also aborts, including a change from 20 to 21 that leaves a 5/10/20 median unchanged (`:589–605,616–625`; test `:368–430`). A writer racing after the last check can still escape these checks. The ADR expressly requires exclusive coordination, stopping the timer, and letting an active job finish; it does not claim the local lock prevents such a race. Recovery preserves evidence and reconciles affected quotes before resumption.

3. **A send lands but the CLI fails or readback cannot establish the expected result.** A file-and-directory-fsynced marker precedes the one-shot send, and matching readback precedes its removal (`:458–502,600–606,627–635`). A retained marker prevents the next cycle from reaching manifest or network work. Neither an unsuccessful CLI exit nor the next timer firing authorizes replay. Manual recovery requires stopping scheduling, ensuring no active job, and establishing and recording disposition against public transaction/chain state. The marker deliberately provides uncertainty custody, not a complete transaction ledger.

4. **A later operation fails after earlier publications succeeded.** Earlier source updates are not rolled back; the cycle is not atomic across twelve publications and four refreshes. The failure table and consequences disclose this. A later fresh cycle after an unambiguous pre-send refusal is distinct from replaying an operation whose marker remains unresolved. Failed sends do not gain an automatic retry path through either the service or the script.

5. **A weekend, stopped host, identity mismatch, or configuration drift leaves stale prices.** The timer does not promise night/weekend service or persistent catch-up. Pin and readback refusals favor safety over availability. Operators must restore independently verified original configuration or obtain a decision for changed scope, rather than widening freshness bounds or pins. The half-hour schedule is a working-hours convenience, not proof of continuous freshness or satisfaction of ADR 0008’s production keeper and TTL obligations.
